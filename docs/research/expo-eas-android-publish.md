# Shipping an Expo app to Google Play in 2026 with no local Android SDK

Research date: 2026-08-25. Scope: cloud-only build and submit via EAS, the Google
Play new-personal-account gate that blocks a fast Shipaton launch, plus Expo web
export, hosting for a live URL and Expo Router API routes as a backend. Every claim
is cited. Where a fact could not be confirmed against a primary source it is marked
UNVERIFIED.

## TL;DR decisions

- The build machine needs no Android SDK. EAS Build compiles the AAB in the cloud
  and EAS manages signing, so the whole binary pipeline is automatable here.
- The real gate is not the build. A personal Google Play account created after
  2023-11-13 must run a closed test with at least 12 testers opted in continuously
  for 14 days before it can even apply for production, then production review is
  usually up to 7 days. That is the critical path, not the code.
- Publicly live on Google Play from a standing start on Aug 25 is roughly a
  three-week job at best (about Sep 15 to Sep 19). It is gated on human steps we
  cannot automate: account creation, identity verification, the $25 fee and real
  testers on real devices.
- The web build is the fast lane. `expo export --platform web` plus `eas deploy`
  gives a live URL in minutes with no account gate. Expo Router API routes give
  a lightweight backend on the same deploy. If Shipaton needs a live, judge-clickable
  product before Play approval lands, the web deploy is the thing to lean on.

## 1. The critical-path gate: Google Play new personal account testing rule

This is the single most important fact for scheduling a launch.

Personal Google Play Console accounts created after 2023-11-13 must run a closed
test before their app is eligible for production distribution. The requirement is a
minimum of 12 testers who have been opted in continuously for at least 14 days.
"Continuously" is literal: if a tester opts out and rejoins, the 14 days must be
consecutive to count, so a tester dropping on day 10 resets that tester.
Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465

After the 14-day window you apply for production from the Play Console Dashboard by
clicking "Apply for production" and answering three sections, titled "About your
closed test", "About your app/game" and "About your production readiness". Google
then reviews and emails the account owner. That review "usually takes seven days or
less, but can occasionally take longer".
Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465

Account setup facts (2026). A full-distribution personal account requires a legal
name and address, a contact email, a phone number, identity verification that can
include a government-issued ID, plus a one-time $25 fee. Organizations additionally
need a D-U-N-S number. These are the values our contact card should fill.
Source: https://android.gadgethacks.com/news/google-android-sideloading-rules-2026-changes-explained/

Separate 2026 change, do not confuse it with the above. Google is rolling out a
developer-verification requirement for distributing apps on certified Android
devices, taking effect from September 2026 starting in Brazil, Indonesia, Singapore
and Thailand, with a broader "papers" deadline around 2026-09-30. This is about
distribution to certified devices in general, not the Play Console closed-test gate,
but a brand-new account in one of the early countries could hit extra verification
friction. Treat the exact interaction between the two as UNVERIFIED for our specific
country of registration.
Sources: https://www.businessworld.in/article/google-arms-android-against-scammers-with-a-24-hour-lock-598463 ,
https://reclaimthenet.org/google-starts-asking-for-developers-papers-on-september-30 ,
https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2026/03/a-new-era-for-choice-and-openness.html

## 2. EAS Build: eas.json profiles and building the AAB in the cloud

EAS Build compiles apps for distribution in the cloud and handles app signing for
Expo and React Native projects out of the box, so no local Android SDK is needed.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/build/introduction/

The config file `eas.json` sits next to `package.json` and is generated by
`eas build:configure`. Build profiles live under the `build` key. The default
config has three profiles and profile names are arbitrary.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/build/eas-json/

```json
{
  "build": {
    "development": { "developmentClient": true, "distribution": "internal" },
    "preview": { "distribution": "internal" },
    "production": {}
  }
}
```

- `development`: depends on `expo-dev-client`, `distribution: internal`, never
  submitted to a store.
- `preview`: no dev tools, internal distribution, meant for production-like internal
  testing. APK is recommended here.
- `production`: store-bound, empty object by default. If you skip `--profile`, EAS
  defaults to `production`.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/build/eas-json/

Output format. The default file format from EAS Build for Android is an Android App
Bundle (`.aab`), which is what Google Play wants. An AAB cannot be installed on a
device or emulator. To get an installable file for sideloading you set
`"buildType": "apk"` under the profile's `android` object, but a `.apk` cannot be
submitted to Play.
Sources: https://docs.expo.dev/build-reference/apk , https://docs.expo.dev/deploy/build-project/

Profiles support `extends` (chain up to depth 5) and per-profile `env` vars that are
applied both when evaluating `app.config.js` and on the builder.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/build/eas-json/

Command to produce the store AAB in the cloud:

```sh
eas build --platform android --profile production
```

AUTOMATABLE HERE. This runs headless on the cloud builder. Login uses `eas login`
interactively or an `EXPO_TOKEN` env var for non-interactive CI.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/submit/android/

## 3. Google-managed app signing

EAS stores a keystore (private key plus public certificate) and signs the AAB or APK
with the keystore linked to the app. EAS can generate this upload keystore for you.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/app-signing/app-credentials/

The recommended model is Play App Signing. You sign uploads with an upload
certificate and "Google Play will automatically replace it with the app signing
certificate". The upside: "if your upload keystore is lost or compromised, you can
contact the Google Play support team to reset the key". On the first Play upload you
see the "App signing by Google Play" notice ("Google is protecting your app signing
key"), enabled by default, then you press "Continue". From EAS's side there is no
difference between signing with an upload certificate or an app signing key.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/app-signing/app-credentials/

Practical takeaway: let EAS generate and hold the upload keystore, let Google manage
the app signing key. AUTOMATABLE HERE except pressing "Continue" on the one-time Play
Console notice, which is a HUMAN step inside the Console UI.

## 4. EAS Submit to Google Play

EAS Submit uploads the binary to Play and places it in a track (internal, alpha, beta
or production). For a brand-new app the default `eas submit` creates the first
release on the internal testing track. The app stays in draft in Play Console
until the store listing and setup tasks are complete.
Sources: https://docs.expo.dev/submit/android/ , https://docs.expo.dev/deploy/submit-to-app-stores/

Prerequisites (from the submit guide):
- A paid Google Play Developer account.
- An app created via "Create app" in Play Console.
- A Google Service Account key uploaded to EAS ("EAS requires a Google Service
  Account key to submit on your behalf"), added via the EAS dashboard under
  Credentials > Android > Service Credentials or `eas credentials --platform android`.
- `android.package` set in app config, for example
  `"android": { "package": "com.yourcompany.yourapp" }`.
- EAS CLI installed and authed: `npm install --global eas-cli && eas login`.
Sources: https://docs.expo.dev/submit/android/ ,
https://github.com/expo/fyi/blob/main/creating-google-service-account.md

Commands:

```sh
eas submit --platform android                       # walks through build selection
eas build --platform android --auto-submit          # build then submit in one go
eas submit --platform android --profile production   # non-interactive, CI-friendly
```

Source: https://docs.expo.dev/submit/android/

Submit profile fields in `eas.json` (Android), from the schema reference:
- `serviceAccountKeyPath` (string): path to the JSON Service Account key used to
  authenticate with Google Play.
- `track` (enum): `production`, `beta`, `alpha`, `internal`.
- `releaseStatus` (enum): `completed`, `draft`, `halted`, `inProgress`.
- `rollout` (number 0 to 1): initial fraction of users eligible; works only with
  `inProgress`.
- `changesNotSentForReview` (boolean, default false): changes are not reviewed until
  explicitly sent for review from the Play Console UI.
- `applicationId` (string): used when accessing an Expo-managed Service Account key.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/eas/json/

CI note: running submit from CI needs `EXPO_TOKEN` set so it runs non-interactively.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/submit/android/

Automatable vs human: uploading the AAB to a track is AUTOMATABLE HERE. Creating the
app in Play Console, creating the Google Cloud Service Account and granting it Play
Console permissions, then completing the store listing are HUMAN steps in the Console
and Cloud UIs the first time. The Service Account creation is a documented one-time
click-through.
Source: https://github.com/expo/fyi/blob/main/creating-google-service-account.md

## 5. Expo web: export, a live URL and API routes as a backend

### Export the web build

```sh
npx expo export --platform web
```

`--platform` defaults to `all` and web is available "if configured in the app
config". All static files are copied into a `dist` directory that "can be served from
a static host". The output dir is `--output-dir` (default `dist`). The contents
of the `public` directory are copied into `dist` as-is. For a sub-path host, set
`experiments.baseUrl` in the app config (for example `"baseUrl": "/my-root"`).
Note the docs show `--platform web` for `export`, not a short `-p web` form; `-p` is
documented only on the run/compile commands, so use `--platform web` for export.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/more/expo-cli/

### web.output mode decides what you get

`web.output` in app config takes three values:
- `single` (default): a single-page app, one `index.html`, no statically indexable
  HTML.
- `static`: pre-renders an HTML file for every route in the `app/` directory (Expo
  Router apps only).
- `server`: outputs static HTML plus API Routes for hosting with a Node.js server.
  This is the one required for API routes.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/versions/latest/config/app/

### Deploy for a live URL: EAS Hosting

EAS Hosting deploys Expo Router and React Native web projects and integrates with the
Expo CLI to automate the deployment of API routes, server functions and server-side
assets. Flow:

```sh
npx expo export --platform web
eas deploy                       # publish; the CLI prints a preview URL
eas deploy:alias --prod --id=<deploymentId>   # promote or roll back production
```

Deployments are immutable and each gets its own preview URL. Custom domains are on
paid plans (one per project, apex or subdomain). Runtime is Cloudflare Workers on V8
with partial Node.js support, so full Node.js compatibility is not available. Static
assets default to a 3600s browser cache. API routes can set `Cache-Control`.
The exact live hostname format (for example an `expo.app` subdomain) is UNVERIFIED
from the intro doc; the doc only guarantees the CLI prints a preview URL.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/eas/hosting/introduction/

Alternative: because `expo export` writes a plain `dist` of static files, a
`single` or `static` web build can go on any static host (GitHub Pages, Netlify,
Vercel, Cloudflare Pages, S3). API routes need a server target, so a static host
covers only the front end. AUTOMATABLE HERE end to end for both routes.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/more/expo-cli/

### Expo Router API routes as a lightweight backend

API routes are server functions that run when a route matches, good for hiding API
keys or custom server logic. They are files in the `app` directory with a `+api.ts`
extension. A file `hello+api.ts` handles `/hello`.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/router/web/api-routes/

```ts
// app/hello+api.ts
export function GET(request: Request) {
  return Response.json({ hello: 'world' });
}

export async function POST(request: Request) {
  const body = await request.json();
  return Response.json({ ok: true, body });
}
```

You can export `GET`, `POST`, `PUT`, `PATCH`, `DELETE`, `HEAD` and `OPTIONS`.
Unsupported methods return `405: Method not allowed`. Dynamic segments use a
bracketed filename like `[post]+api.ts`, with the handler reading params from the
second argument. Filenames cannot use platform-specific extensions (`hello+api.web.ts`
will not work). Secrets in a `+api.ts` file are not bundled into the client.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/router/web/api-routes/

Requirement and deploy: set `"web": { "output": "server" }` in app config, build
with `npx expo export --platform web` (or `--no-ssg` for server-only), then deploy
"using EAS Hosting or another third-party service", with adapters for Bun, Express,
Netlify and Vercel.
Source: https://docs.expo.dev/router/web/api-routes/

## 6. Day-by-day timeline, Aug 25 to publicly live

The hard floor is the 14 continuous days of closed testing with 12 testers, which
cannot be compressed. On top of it sit account verification at the front and
production review at the back. So the earliest a brand-new personal account can be
publicly live on Play is roughly: (day 12 testers are opted in) + 14 days + review
of up to 7 days. From an Aug 25 start that lands in mid-to-late September, not sooner.

Legend: [AUTO] automatable on this machine. [HUMAN] needs a person (account,
identity, banking, the $25, real device testing, Console UI clicks).

| Day | Date | Step | Who |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | Aug 25 | Create Google Play developer account, pay the one-time $25, start identity verification | [HUMAN] |
| 0 | Aug 25 | Scaffold Expo app, write `eas.json` (production profile), set `android.package` | [AUTO] |
| 0 | Aug 25 | Kick first cloud AAB: `eas build --platform android --profile production` | [AUTO] |
| 0-1 | Aug 25-26 | Deploy the web build for a live URL now: `expo export --platform web` then `eas deploy` | [AUTO] |
| 1-3 | Aug 26-28 | Identity verification clears (duration UNVERIFIED, plan 1 to 3 days, can be longer) | [HUMAN] |
| ~2 | Aug 27 | Create the app in Play Console, complete store listing, data safety, content rating | [HUMAN] |
| ~2 | Aug 27 | Create Google Cloud Service Account, grant Play Console access, upload key to EAS | [HUMAN] |
| ~2-3 | Aug 27-28 | Submit AAB to the closed testing track: `eas submit --platform android` | [AUTO] |
| ~2-3 | Aug 27-28 | First-app review on a new account clears so the closed build goes live (up to ~7 days, often faster, UNVERIFIED) | [HUMAN wait] |
| ~3 | Aug 28 | Recruit 12+ testers, they opt in via the tester link and install on real devices | [HUMAN] |
| 3 -> 17 | Aug 28 -> Sep 11 | Keep 12+ testers opted in continuously for 14 days (the fixed floor) | [HUMAN wait] |
| ~17 | Sep 11 | Apply for production: answer the three Console sections | [HUMAN] |
| 17-24 | Sep 11-18 | Production access review, usually 7 days or less | [HUMAN wait] |
| ~24 | Sep 18-19 | Roll out to production, app publicly live | [AUTO submit, HUMAN release] |

Best case (fast verification, testers opted in by Aug 26, fast reviews): publicly
live around Sep 14 to Sep 16. Realistic case: Sep 18 to Sep 20. Slippage risk is
concentrated in the two review waits and in any tester dropping inside the 14-day
window, which resets that tester's clock.
Sources for the gated steps: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465 ,
https://docs.expo.dev/submit/android/

What is automatable here end to end: the AAB build, signing (EAS-managed upload key),
the submit upload to any track, the web export, the web deploy and the API-routes
backend. What is not: account creation, identity and banking verification, paying the
$25, creating the app and Service Account in the Console the first time, recruiting
real testers on real devices, plus clicking through the Console review applications.

Implication for Shipaton: if the deadline is before mid-September, a fully public
Play production listing from a new personal account is not realistically achievable,
because 14 continuous testing days plus review will not fit. The web deploy (live URL
plus API routes) is the piece that can be genuinely live and judge-clickable within a
day. The internal or closed testing track can show a real installable Android
build well before production access is granted. Plan the submission around those two
if the calendar is tight.
